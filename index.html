The gap
⟨⟩

The missing piece between paid and delivered.

Payment infrastructure today is exceptional at moving money. Stripe, banks, and stablecoin rails settle billions every day, reliably and at scale. What they don't do — and were never designed to do — is encode the conditions around a payment.

So every platform, marketplace, and builder ends up writing the same logic from scratch: when should funds release? What counts as delivery? Who signs off? What happens if no one does? It's the same puzzle, solved badly, a thousand times.

Peyya is that missing piece. The logic layer that sits on top of the rails you already use.

What Peyya is

Three capabilities. One primitive.

Every Peyya flow — whether it settles a construction milestone or an agent's API call — composes from the same building blocks.

① Conditional

Conditional payments

Funds release when conditions are met — a signature, a delivery, a deadline, an outside event. You define the logic. Peyya executes it, exactly once, without a middleman deciding what counts.

② Agentic

Agent-to-agent commerce

Native support for x402, A2A, AP2, MCP, and ACP. Agents can earn, spend, and split revenue — autonomously, across protocols, without a human approving each transaction.

③ Non-custodial

Non-custodial by design

Peyya never touches the money. We add the logic on top of licensed, regulated infrastructure — so you get programmable control without taking on custody, compliance, or settlement risk. You own the logic. The rails own the rest.

On proof, not promise

Payment on proof, not on trust.

Traditionally, money moves when someone trusts that the other side will deliver — or when a middleman holds the funds and decides who's right.

Both models break down when the other side is an agent. And both leave contractors, marketplaces, and platforms writing brittle custody logic they were never meant to own.

With Peyya, the payment and the proof are the same moment. Funds move when the agreed thing actually happens — a signed approval, a delivered result, a confirmation from the other agent. Money moves against evidence, not against a promise.

The result: no custody risk for you, no trust gap for the counterparty, no brittle middleware between them.
